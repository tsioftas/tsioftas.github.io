Privacy policy — Щасливої Дороги

Last updated: 14 October 2026.

The game collects nothing that identifies you. It sends a summary of how each run and each session of play went, under a random number made for your copy of the game, so the game can be balanced and improved. The one advert in it is shown by Google, and Google does collect data about you.

The advert

After a crash the game may offer a second chance in return for watching a video advertisement. It is only ever offered, never shown unasked, and turning it down costs you nothing but the run.

That advert is served by Google AdMob. To choose and show one, and to count that it was shown, Google's SDK in the game uses your device's advertising ID, your IP address, and basic information about the device and the app. How Google uses that is described at https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.

If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, the game asks for your consent through Google's own form before any advert is requested, and works fully whatever you answer. You can change your answer at any time under Settings → Ad privacy in the game. You can reset or delete your advertising ID at any time in Android's settings (Settings → Privacy → Ads).

For the advert, Google's SDK asks Android for network access (INTERNET, ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE), for the advertising ID (AD_ID), for basic phone state (READ_BASIC_PHONE_STATE — such as the type of mobile network and the software version; not your calls, contacts or number), and to do its own work in the background (WAKE_LOCK, FOREGROUND_SERVICE). These are all granted at install without a prompt; the game asks for nothing else, and nothing that would show you one.

How the game is played

So we can see how the game is really played and improve it, it sends two kinds of summary to a server of our own:

When a run ends, a summary of the run:

  • how far you drove, for how long, and what ended the run;
  • the money earned, paid out and fined in the game;
  • the passengers you carried and how their mood went;
  • the car, its parts and the companions in it;
  • the device it ran on: its model, operating system, graphics chip, screen size and refresh rate.

When you leave the game (it closes or goes to the background), a summary of that session:

  • how long you spent on each screen, and which buttons you pressed;
  • how many runs you started and how they ended, including a run you left unfinished and how far it had got;
  • whether the advert was offered and taken, and what you bought in the garage;
  • where you stand overall: distance driven, money, endings collected, legs of the journey done, the car, parts and companions you have, your language and sound settings, and how many times you have started the game;
  • the device it ran on, the same as in a run's summary.

Each summary carries the day (the date only, not the time), the version of the game that sent it, and a random number made the first time you start the game. It is kept with your progress on your device, and lets us tell how one copy of the game is played over time: whether people come back, and where they stop. It is not your name, account, phone number, device ID or advertising ID, and it is tied to none of them. Uninstalling the game deletes it; a new install gets a new one. No location is sent. Your IP address reaches the server only because every message on the internet carries one; it is not stored or logged.

Summaries are kept on Cloudflare's servers (Cloudflare Workers and D1), are not shared with or sold to anyone, and are used for nothing but improving the game. Each summary is kept for at most a year; after that only each day's totals remain (how many copies of the game were played, how many runs and sessions, and how far they drove), which say nothing about any one of them. We collect them in our legitimate interest in making the game work well; if you object, write to the address below. A summary made offline is sent the next time the device is online.

What is stored, and where

Your progress — your best run, the money you have banked, the car you drive, the finales you have collected, the settings you have chosen — is written to a file in the game's own private storage on your device. Nobody but the game can read it, and the game never sends it anywhere. If your phone backs itself up to your Google account — Android's own backup, which you switch on or off in your phone's settings — that file is part of the backup, so reinstalling the game or moving to a new phone brings your progress back. The backup goes to your Google account, not to us, and nothing else of the game's is in it. Without it, uninstalling the game deletes your progress.

The game has no account, no sign-in and no purchases.

Children

The game is made for a general audience and is not directed at children.

A note on how this is kept true rather than merely stated: the game's own code makes one kind of network call, in src/Uplink.gd, which sends the run and session summaries above and nothing else. Everything else on the network is Google's SDK, confined to src/AdMob.gd and loaded on Android only. The permissions the SDK brings are pinned by a test (tests/test_adverts.gd) and by the build script, which refuses to produce a build that asks for any permission not listed in store/permissions.txt. The server that receives the summaries is in server/usage/: it stores each one and keeps nothing about the sender.
